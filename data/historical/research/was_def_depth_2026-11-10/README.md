# Washington Commanders defensive depth, 2026 Weeks 1–9

Research only, compiled 2026-11-10 (America/New_York). No picks and no model changes. This answers whether the injured / IR defenders were starters and who took their snaps ahead of Thursday Night Football on 2026-11-12 at the Giants.

Washington is 2-5-1. Week 7 was a bye. The games after Daron Payne and Javon Kinlaw last played are Week 8 (2026-11-01 vs Philadelphia, L 10–27) and Week 9 (2026-11-08 vs the Rams, L 13–20). That is a two-game sample, not one. Season-ending surgery for both linemen was reported Nov 4, which falls between those two games; both were already inactive for Week 8.

NFL Savant's weekly usage file and the public nflverse snap-count release agree exactly on every Commanders defender-week (snaps and snap percentage). Official starting lineups were retrieved only for Week 9, from the official gamebook in GhostPapii21/nfl-matchup-database on main (commit a6e5475). Weeks 1–8 official start counts are a gap; starter language for those weeks is based on snap share and NFL Savant's role label.

Position labels are taken from NFL Savant usage (DT, DE, LB, CB, S), the nflverse weekly roster `depth_chart_position` (DT, DE, ILB, OLB, SS, FS, CB), the Week 9 official gamebook (DT, DE, OLB, LB, CB, S), and the Nov 10 ESPN 3-4 depth chart in the screenshots. They do not always agree. The important mismatches are called out below.

## Injured and IR defenders

### Daron Payne, DT — yes, he was a starter

Payne was Washington's lead defensive tackle. NFL Savant lists him as a starter at 73.8% of defensive snaps over 6 games (292 snaps). He led the team's DT snap share every week he played: 62, 67, 72, 86, 71, and 85 percent in Weeks 1–6. Last game: Week 6 at San Francisco, 2026-10-19, 53 snaps (85%). While he was playing: 7 solo tackles, 7 assists, 2 TFL, 1 sack, 5 QB hits, 3 PBP run-stuffs.

He did not play Week 8 (injury report: Out, foot; roster inactive) and was on IR (RES) for Week 9. The brief's season-ending foot surgery date of Nov 4 sits between those two games.

Because Payne and Kinlaw came off the field in the same window, the interior snaps were redistributed to the same group of replacements. You cannot cleanly say "Settle replaced Payne" and "Tuttle replaced Kinlaw." Together they had been playing most of the interior snaps.

### Javon Kinlaw, DT — yes, he was a starter

Kinlaw was the other starting tackle. NFL Savant lists him as a starter at 60.2% over the same 6 games (239 snaps). He held 56–80% in Weeks 1–5, then only 44% (27 snaps) in Week 6 when he was questionable with an ankle and knee. That Week 6 game is his last appearance. While he was playing: 8 solo, 5 assists, 2 TFL, 1 sack, 3 QB hits, 5 PBP run-stuffs, 2 passes defensed. Week 8: Out (knee), did not practice, inactive. Week 9: IR.

ESPN's current chart lists him as the RDE on injured reserve. Every other source in this report — Savant, nflverse roster (DT), PFR snaps, and the gamebook — labels him a defensive tackle. Treat the ESPN slot as a 3-4 diagram label, not a change in his actual position.

### Javontae Jean-Baptiste, OLB — no, he was not a starter

Situational edge. 22 defensive snaps on the snap chart all season: 9 (16%) in Week 1 and 13 (24%) in Week 2. Inactive Weeks 3–4, active in Week 5 with 0 defensive snaps, then on reserve from Week 6 (pectoral; the brief dates the IR to Oct 19, which is also the Week 6 game date). ESPN lists him as an SLB reserve behind Armstrong and Chaisson. His absence did not force a new starter; that edge rotation was already Chaisson, Oweh, and Armstrong.

One source discrepancy: nflverse official weekly stats credit him with 2 solo tackles in Week 5 despite 0 defensive snaps in the snap chart. That may be special-teams tackles sitting in the defensive stat fields, or unlisted snaps. It is flagged, not resolved.

### Leo Chenal, ILB — rotational, with one starter-level game

NFL Savant role: rotational, 49.3% over 3 games (84 snaps). Snap line: 42% in Week 1, 82% in Week 2, 16 snaps (24%) in Week 3, then gone. The 82% game came while Frankie Luvu was out with a groin injury (Out, Weeks 2–3). Chenal left the Week 3 game early (2026-09-27 vs Seattle). The brief dates his IR to Sep 29, two days later, for a neck injury. The nflverse injury file lists his Week 3 practice injury as chest, not neck — that wording discrepancy is unresolved here. Roster depth chart position: ILB. Production in those 3 games: 9 solo, 9 assists, 1 TFL, 3 PBP run-stuffs.

He was not the every-down middle linebacker. That is Sonny Styles (92.8%) and, when healthy, Luvu (91%). After Chenal went on IR, Luvu returned in Week 4 at 95% and has been every-down since. Kain Medrano had a temporary spike (66% in Week 3, 32% in Week 4) and then receded.

### Shy Tuttle, DT — was not a starter, is one now, and is questionable for Thursday

Rotational tackle for Weeks 1–6 (typically 20–48%; season line 38.7% over 8 games, 209 snaps). After Payne and Kinlaw went out he was elevated: 46% in Week 8, then 57% in Week 9 — his season high — and he is in the official Week 9 starting lineup at DT next to Tim Settle (gamebook: S.Tuttle, DT #96). In Weeks 8–9 he had 5 solo, 5 assists, 3 TFL, 1 sack, and 2 PBP run-stuffs, including 4 combined tackles and 1 TFL in the Week 9 gamebook.

ESPN now lists him as the starting RDE and questionable. The Nov 10 screenshots are the source for the questionable tag; nflverse's injury file only has a Week 9 limited practice (shoulder) and does not yet have a Week 10 report. If he cannot go Thursday, the next interior bodies with evidence are Settle, Jer'Zhan Newton (20 snaps in Week 9, just activated off IR), and Ricky Barber (15 snaps in Week 9, practice-squad elevation). That is a one-game sample for Newton and Barber. Omenihu and Wise are defensive ends, not interior replacements.

## Other notable lineup changes (not from this IR group)

- **Mike Sainristil is no longer the nickel.** He played 98% and 82% in Weeks 1–2, then 39, 8, 48, 11, 0, and 1 percent. ESPN now lists him as the backup nickel behind Amik Robertson. He has been active every week; this is a usage change, not an injury.
- **Trey Amos is the new starting corner.** He was on reserve Weeks 1–5, played 58% in Week 6 after being activated, then 87% and 97%, and started Week 9. **Rasul Douglas** rose to 85–98% in Weeks 4–5 and has since fallen to 46% and 24%. ESPN still lists Douglas as the starting LCB. That does not match the Week 8–9 snaps or the Week 9 starting lineup (Amos and Robertson started; Douglas was a substitute).
- **Nick Cross missed Weeks 3–5 with an illness** and has been every-down since returning (89–100%). **Quan Martin** played starter-level snaps (87–99%) while Cross was out, then receded to 29–56%. ESPN lists Martin as the FS backup behind Jeremy Reaves. Reaves started Week 9 but played only 60%, his low for the year.
- **Tyler Owens is listed by ESPN as the SS backup. He has 0 defensive snaps this season.** His usage is special teams. Do not treat him as Cross's defensive replacement on current evidence.
- **Charles Omenihu** (DE) missed Weeks 5–6 (groin/ankle), then returned at 67% and 76% and started Week 9. That matters when you look at the defensive line before vs after Payne and Kinlaw: Omenihu was also missing at the end of the "before" window and present in both "after" games. **Deatrich Wise** picked up DE snaps from Week 5 on.
- **Jer'Zhan Newton** came off IR for Week 9 only (27%). **Ricky Barber** was a practice-squad elevation for Week 9 (20%). **Byron Cowart** was a practice-squad elevation for Week 8 only (16%) and was back on the practice squad for Week 9.

Week 9 official starting defense, from the gamebook: OLB Oweh, DE Omenihu, DT Tuttle, DT Settle, OLB Chaisson, LB Styles, LB Luvu, CB Amos, CB Robertson, S Reaves, S Cross.

ESPN vs that lineup: most of the 3-4 starters match recent usage, with two exceptions. ESPN lists Armstrong as starting SLB, but Chaisson started Week 9 and has the higher season snap share (68.3% vs 49.7%). ESPN lists Douglas as starting LCB, but he played 24% in Week 9 and did not start. ESPN also puts Tuttle and Kinlaw at RDE; the other sources all say DT.

## Run defense and pass defense before vs after Payne and Kinlaw

There are **two** post-Payne/Kinlaw games (Weeks 8 and 9), not one. Week 7 was a bye. Official opponent-offense numbers (nflverse team-week stats) and PBP rates:

Game-by-game official rush yards allowed: 136 (PHI), 66 (DAL), 44 (SEA), 128 (IND), 103 (NYG), 126 (SF), then 91 (PHI) and 124 (LA). The Week 9 gamebook matches the 124 yards on 29 carries (4.3) and 20 points allowed.

The two-game "after" averages show fewer points allowed, slightly fewer yards per carry, and better pass EPA, with slightly more rush yards per game. That is not a finding that the defense improved because the starters went down, and it is not a finding that it got worse. The sample is two games. Week 8 against Philadelphia (10 points, 91 rush yards, 5 sacks) dominates the averages. Omenihu also returned in that same window, Amos became a starting corner, and the opponents changed. Do not treat this split as a clean Payne/Kinlaw effect.

Small counting-stat discrepancies exist between PBP and official team stats (for example Week 9 pass attempts: PBP 42, official/gamebook 43). Official team-week numbers are the counting source; PBP is used for EPA, success, and stuff rate.

## What this means, in plain words, for Thursday against the Giants

On the defensive line, the two players who were actually starting tackles — Payne and Kinlaw — are gone for the season. The veterans who have taken those snaps are Tim Settle, who was a rotational tackle (about 31–44%) and then played 61% and 44% in Weeks 8–9, and Shy Tuttle, who was also rotational and then started Week 9 at 57%. Tuttle is the one listed as questionable. If he sits, Washington would be asking Settle plus two players with a single game of regular-season defensive snaps this year (Newton and Barber) to handle the interior. That is a real depth drop relative to Weeks 1–6. It is also only two games of evidence since the starters went out, and those two games do not show a collapse in official rushing yards allowed (91 and 124, versus a 100.5-yard average when Payne and Kinlaw played).

The edge and inside linebacker IR cases do not change the starting group in the same way. Jean-Baptiste was a 16–24% situational piece. Chenal was a rotational ILB who had one big game while Luvu was hurt; Luvu has been back and every-down since Week 4, with Styles still the other every-down linebacker. The edge starters in the Week 9 gamebook were Oweh and Chaisson, which is also who led the team in pressures.

In the secondary, the bigger change is not an IR defender. Sainristil is no longer playing every-down nickel, Amos has taken over a starting corner job, and Douglas's snaps have declined even though ESPN still lists Douglas as a starter. Cross is back from the illness that kept him out of Weeks 3–5. None of that comes from Payne, Kinlaw, Chenal, or Jean-Baptiste, but it is the current Week 10 secondary, not the Week 1 secondary.

This is a description of who has been on the field. It is not a projection or a bet, and the model remains blocked for projections.
