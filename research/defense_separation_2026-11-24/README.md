# Defenses that take away receiver separation (Nov 24, 2026)

Owner's question after MNF (Washington blanketed Chase and Higgins): is limiting separation a defense trait we can key on?
`python3 study.py` -> `results.json`. Next Gen Stats weekly separation 2018-2026; each receiver's gap vs his own prior
average; a defense's figure = target-weighted mean gap it allowed in earlier weeks.

- **Trait: weak.** 2,444 defense-weeks: correlation of a defense's earlier figure with this week's 0.115 (slope 0.26).
  Mostly game plan and matchup, not a stable habit.
- **80-yard games: no gain.** Added to the star model, held out 2025-26: receivers z 0.7, WR1s z 1.0, neither passes.
  WR1s by defense third (2025-26): takes away separation 66.6 yds / 27.6% 80+; middle 51.9 / 22.6%; gives 66.2 / 32.2%.
- **Washington is not that defense:** 2026 receivers get +0.28 yards more separation vs WAS than usual (5th most). The
  Chase plan was game-specific (USA Today: Daronte Jones' "pressure-happy, Brian Flores-inspired scheme... contain both
  Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins while forcing others to beat them"). Takes away the most in 2026: HOU -0.44, IND -0.43,
  DEN -0.34, CIN -0.27, DET -0.24.

Use: context on the board (not applied). Better early signs of a Chase-type plan: blitz-heavy coordinator (2026 FTN
rates, PR #118), shadow-corner pattern and QB results vs heavy blitzing (Tuesday review). Research only.
