## Repeatable vs likely to change
**Repeatable:**
- **Metcalf's volume.** He has a 25% target share over the last 3 and 93% snaps, and saw 9 targets in game one. CLE's outside CB room is banged up (Harden LP, Campbell status unknown). The volume gate passes. His yards still swing, so use middle rungs.
- **Warren's workload.** He has 68% of carries, 75% snaps over the last 3, and about 15 carries a game. As the home favorite in a run-heavy streak, more carries are likely than the 17 he had in game one.
- **CLE's target concentration.** Boston (22%), Concepcion (18–22%) and Fannin (17%) are role-driven. Judkins' 7 targets were tied to the trailing and checkdown script.

**Likely to change:**
- **Rodgers' 40 attempts and 299 yards** were a trailing-script product. PIT is favored now. Per 10G, PIT pass volume is lower if they lead, so avoid Rodgers passing-yard overs (Dak rule).
- **Boston's 89 yards** included one 60-yard play against Everette. Don't anchor on it. The CLE OL is depleted (Howard, Jenkins DNP) against a fresh Watt and Heyward, which means pressure risk, shorter drops, and favors Concepcion and Fannin on short work over deep shots.
- **CLE run game.** Judkins went 17-53 (3.1 YPC) in game one, and PIT's run defense has since tightened (18 rush yds allowed to NO). This fails the "can the offense move the ball" guardrail. Cap Judkins' yards and take no RB TD legs.
- **R. Wilson 74 yds on 31% snaps** was a one-off explosive (a 49-yarder). His snap and target role doesn't support repeating it. Bernard now has a 25% target share over the last 3 (up from game one's 2 targets), and that role has grown.
