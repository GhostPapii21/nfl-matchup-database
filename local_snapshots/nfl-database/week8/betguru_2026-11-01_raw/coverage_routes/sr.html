          How the matchup grade is calculated

          Every qualified WR against his upcoming opponent's projected primary defender, ranked by a weekly matchup score. The base of that score is 75% this week's PPR projection and 25% the WR's target-efficiency line (once he clears a pooled-target minimum); that base is then adjusted by three multipliers -- matchup (up to ±10%, from how well the primary defender's coverage fits the WR, his alignment, and how much the defense funnels targets his way), surge (up to +5%, from projected targets above his per-game average) and role (up to ±7.5%, from his first-read share, targets-per-route and red-zone target share). The Grade column shows each WR's score as a share of the best score on the slate, so the top matchup always grades 100.

          How the primary defender is chosen

          The primary defender is picked by alignment mirroring: a WR's left-wide rate is matched against the opponent CB with the most snaps at right cornerback, and so on for right-wide and slot. One spot per receiver on a team, so teammates are never mirrored onto the same defender.

          How to use WR/CB matchups in fantasy football

          What a good matchup looks like

          A high matchup grade means the receiver's projection, his target efficiency and the coverage he faces point the same way. Read three columns together: the Grade, the defender's targets and receptions allowed per game, and the defense's man-coverage rate. A defender who allows five or more targets a game on a defense that plays a lot of man coverage is the profile the grade rewards.

          Man vs zone

          Receivers produce differently against man and zone coverage; the Man and Zone columns show fantasy points per route against each. When a receiver's Man number is well above his Zone number, a defense that plays mostly zone takes away his best route tree, and the Man% column tells you how much man coverage that defense plays.

          Slot vs perimeter

          The primary defender follows the receiver's alignment: a slot receiver is matched with the slot cornerback, a left-wide receiver with the right cornerback. When a receiver splits his snaps across alignments, the defender shown is the one across from his most common spot, and some of his routes will run against other defenders.

          When to ignore the matchup

          Volume beats coverage. A receiver with a 30% target share in a hard matchup usually outscores a receiver with a 12% share in an easy one, and the xFP/G column already carries that volume. Use the matchup grade to choose between receivers with similar projections, not to overrule the projection.

          What are the best WR/CB matchups this week (Week 8)?

          On StatRankings' Week 8 board the highest-graded open matchups are Jaxon Smith-Njigba vs Tyrique Stevenson (100), Puka Nacua vs Donte Jackson (91) and CeeDee Lamb vs Denzel Burke (85). Grades compare each receiver's matchup score with the best on the slate, so the top matchup is always 100. Updated October 31, 2026.

          How is a WR/CB matchup grade calculated?

          A receiver's weekly matchup score starts from this week's PPR projection and his target-efficiency line, is adjusted by three multipliers the board calls matchup, surge and role, and is shown as a share of the best score on the slate, so the top matchup always grades 100.

          Does the board track shadow coverage?

          No. StatRankings pairs each wide receiver with the opponent cornerback who most often lines up across from the receiver's main alignment (left, right or slot). It does not predict whether a cornerback will follow one receiver around the field; where a defense does that, the primary defender shown is still the alignment match.

          What is a WR vs CB matchup in fantasy football?

          A WR vs CB matchup compares a wide receiver with the cornerback most likely to cover him in a given week: how often the corner is targeted, what he allows per route, whether his defense plays man or zone, and how the receiver produces against that coverage. Fantasy and DFS players use it to choose between receivers with similar projections.
